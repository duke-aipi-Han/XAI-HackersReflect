01 / The mindset

Beyond
the keyboard.

This book changed how I think about hacking. It’s not just about computers. Sometimes you can follow every rule and still turn a system on its head.

01

Anything with rules can be hacked.

Rewards programs, elections, even everyday habits: once I started looking, I saw opportunities for hacks everywhere.

02

Rules don’t always match the goal.

A company wants more sales but rewards product labels. Someone buys the cheapest labels they can find. That gap is the loophole.

03

Money helps you use the loophole.

A clever idea only gets you so far. Buying every lottery ticket takes money. The people who can afford to act often get the biggest advantage.

04

A hack can change the rules.

When enough people use a loophole, the rules may change. Or the hack becomes so normal that everyone accepts it.

05

AI can find the gaps faster.

AI could find and spread hacks much faster. It could also help us fix the gaps first. I want to understand what it’s trying to achieve and who benefits.

Pudding for miles.

Buy food, collect labels, earn airline miles. Can cheap pudding get you a better deal than a full dinner?

Experiment 02

Draw the districts.

Most voters support Party A. Can Party B still win by changing where the district lines go? This is gerrymandering.

The rules

Make 5 districts of 5 voters each. Squares in a district must join along their sides. The party with more votes wins that district’s seat.

Your goal

Help Party B win at least 3 seats. Move the lines, not the voters.

Redraw the map

Choose a district color, then click squares to add them. A and B show how each person votes.

Same voters, every time

Party A / Party B60% / 40%
Seats won by A / B5 / 0

A has 60% of the votes, so 3 of 5 seats would match its share. B would get 2. Drawing districts can lead to a very different result.

The takeaway

Same voters. Same rules. A different winner. The person drawing the map has a lot of power, even without changing a single vote.

Experiment 03

Buy the lottery.

What if buying every possible number cost less than the prize? You’d need a lot of cash, but you could take luck out of the game.

The rules

Pick from 100 numbers, 00–99. One is drawn at random. Each ticket costs $2. If others win too, you share the prize.

Your goal

Buy every number and come out ahead. Then try a smaller prize or share it with another winner.

The takeaway

Here, $200 buys every number. Win $250 alone and you make $50. Share the prize and you lose money. Having a winning ticket doesn’t always mean coming out ahead.

These games keep things small and simple. Real elections and lotteries are much more complicated.

03 / A different question

When a system gets hacked,
I want to ask more than
“Who found the loophole?”
I want to ask who benefits.

Now I look at who gets the reward and who pays the price. With AI, I want to ask the same questions: what is it trying to do, and can we see how it gets there?
